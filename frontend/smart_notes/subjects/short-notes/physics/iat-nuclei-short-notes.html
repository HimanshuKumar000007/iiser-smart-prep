Hot Topic

1. Core Concept & Nuclear Composition

The nucleus is the ultra-dense center of the atom. In IAT, understanding the relationship between its constituent nucleons and its overall stability is key.

Isotopes

Same Z, Different A.
Example: 1H, 2H, 3H

Isobars

Same A, Different Z.
Example: 3H, 3He

Isotones

Same (A-Z) neutrons.
Example: 14C, 16O

The Density Trick

Nuclear density is independent of Mass Number (A). Whether it's Hydrogen or Uranium, the density is roughly 2.3 × 1017 kg/m3. The ratio of densities of any two nuclei is always **1:1**.

2. Key Formulas

R = R0 A1/3
Nuclear Radius. (where R0 ≈ 1.2 × 10-15 m = 1.2 fm, A = Mass Number).
Δm = [Zmp + (A - Z)mn] - Mnucleus
Mass Defect (Δm).
BE = Δm · c2  |  BE = Δm × 931.5 MeV
Binding Energy. (Shortcut: If Δm is in amu (u), multiply by 931.5).

3. Binding Energy & Stability Curve

Binding Energy per Nucleon (BE/A) is the true measure of a nucleus's stability.

  • Peak Stability: At Iron (56Fe) where BE/A ≈ 8.8 MeV.
  • Middle Range (30 < A < 170): Nuclei are very stable (BE/A ≈ 8.0 - 8.5 MeV).
  • Light Nuclei (A < 30): Low stability; tend to undergo Fusion to move up the curve.
  • Heavy Nuclei (A > 170): Low stability; tend to undergo Fission to move up the curve.

4. The Nuclear Force

  • Short Range: Effective only up to 2-3 fm.
  • Strongest: Much stronger than electromagnetic forces at small ranges.
  • Saturation Property: A nucleon only interacts with its immediate neighbors.
  • Charge Independent: Fpp ≈ Fpn ≈ Fnn.

5. Radioactivity Laws

Radioactivity is a spontaneous, random process. The rate of decay is proportional to the number of nuclei present.

N = N0e-λt
T1/2 = 0.693 / λ

Types of Decay:

  • Alpha (α): AZX → A-4Z-2Y + 42He
  • Beta Minus (β-): n → p + e- + ν̄ (Antineutrino)
  • Beta Plus (β+): p → n + e+ + ν (Neutrino)
  • Gamma (γ): Excited nucleus returns to ground state (No change in A or Z).

6. Nuclear Reactions (Energy)

Feature Fission Fusion
Process Heavy nucleus splitting Light nuclei merging
Energy released Lower per nucleon Higher per nucleon
Fuel Uranium (limited) Hydrogen (abundant)

7. Conceptual Insights

Why Radioactivity?

Unstable nuclei decay randomly and spontaneously entirely to achieve a higher binding energy per nucleon (to shift closer to the Iron peak on the BE curve).

Beta Decay & Neutrinos:

In β- decay, an antineutrino is emitted to conserve lepton number and energy. In β+ decay, a neutrino is emitted. These particles are nearly massless and very hard to detect.

8. Common Mistakes

  • Mass vs Mass Number: Alpha decay reduces A by 4 and Z by 2. Beta decay changes Z but NOT A.
  • Neutrino vs Antineutrino: Antineutrino (ν̄) with β-, Neutrino (ν) with β+.
  • Stable density: Nuclear density doesn't change with A. It's always ~1017 kg/m3.

9. Example Applications

Percentage Decayed vs Remaining

"What fraction of a radioactive sample decays in exactly 3 half-lives?"

Insight: Fraction remaining is N = N0(1/2)3 = N0 / 8. Therefore, the fraction that has decayed = 1 - 1/8 = 7/8 (or 87.5%). Read the question carefully to see if it asks for remaining or decayed!

Simultaneous Decay

If a single radioactive nucleus can decay via two distinct, competing paths with decay constants λ1 and λ2, the overall effective decay constant is exactly λeff = λ1 + λ2. (Therefore, for half-life: 1/Teff = 1/T1 + 1/T2).

10. IAT Exam Focus Points

Radius Ratio Questions:

Expect straightforward questions like, "Find the exact ratio of the radius of 27Al to 125Te."
Answer: R1 / R2 = (27/125)1/3 = 3/5.

Q-Value & Kinetic Energy:

In spontaneous alpha decay of a stationary nucleus, linear momentum is conserved. Because the alpha particle is much lighter than the daughter nucleus, it carries away the vast majority of the released energy. The kinetic energy of the alpha particle is a specific fraction of the total Q-value: Kα = Q × [A / (A - 4)].

Radioactive Dating/Counting:

Use the N = N0(1/2)n shortcut extensively for numericals. It almost entirely avoids complex logarithm calculations in most IAT problems.

Conservation Laws:

In ANY nuclear reaction, the following are strictly conserved: Total Charge (Z), Total Mass Number (A), Linear Momentum, Angular Momentum, and Total Energy (Mass + Energy equivalence).

Nuclear Density Trick:

If a question asks for the ratio of nuclear densities of ANY two elements (e.g., Gold to Oxygen), the answer is always precisely 1:1.

11. Quick Check Quiz

Nuclear Size

Q1: If the radius of 27Al is R, what is the radius of 64Cu?

Correct! R ∝ A1/3. Thus, RCu / RAl = (64/27)1/3 = 4/3.
Radioactivity

Q2: A sample has 16g of material. How much remains after 4 half-lives?

Correct! N = N0(1/2)4 = 16 × 1/16 = 1g.
