Moving Charges and Magnetism

Physics Unit 4
25 min read
IAT Advanced
Hot Topic

1. Core Concept

Moving electric charges (currents) produce a magnetic field in the surrounding space. Conversely, an external magnetic field exerts a force on moving charges and current-carrying wires.

Visual Intuition

Imagine the magnetic field as a whirlpool. A stationary charge sits undisturbed, but a moving charge gets pushed sideways by the flow.

2. Key Formulas

Fm = q(v × B) = qvB sinθ
Magnetic Force on a moving charge.
Fnet = qE + q(v × B)
Lorentz Force (Total force due to electric and magnetic fields).
F = I(L × B) = ILB sinθ
Force on a current-carrying wire of length L.
dB = (μ0I / 4π) (dl × r̂ / r2)
Biot-Savart Law for a small current element.
∮ B · dl = μ0Ienclosed
Ampere's Circuital Law.
m = NIA  |  τ = m × B = mB sinθ
Magnetic Dipole Moment and Torque on a current loop. Direction by Right-Hand Thumb Rule.

3. Important Results

Particle Trajectories in magnetic field:

Circular Motion

(v ⊥ B)

Radius R = mv/qB

Time period T is independent of v and R!

Helical Motion

(v at θ to B)

Pitch P = (v cosθ)T

Spiral path along B-direction.

Magnetic Field due to Specific Geometries:

Geometry Field (B) Notes
Infinite Wire μ0I / 2πd d = perp. distance
Loop Center μ0I / 2R For N turns, ×N
Loop Axis μ0IR2 / 2(x2+R2)3/2 Max at x=0
Ideal Solenoid μ0nI n = N/L (turns/meter)

Force Between Parallel Wires & "The Ampere"

F/L = μ0I1I2 / (2πd)

Definition of Ampere: 1 Ampere is the constant current which, if maintained in two straight parallel conductors of infinite length, placed 1 metre apart in vacuum, would produce a force equal to 2 × 10-7 Newtons per metre of length.

Memory Trick: Like currents ATTRACT (same direction). Opposite currents repel. (Opposite of electrostatics!)

4. Conceptual Insights

Zero Work Done

The magnetic force is always perpendicular to the velocity (Fm ⊥ v). Therefore, magnetic force does ZERO work. It can change the direction of a particle, but NEVER its speed or kinetic energy.

Ampere vs. Biot-Savart

Ampere's Law is the magnetic equivalent of Gauss's Law in electrostatics. Use it primarily for highly symmetric configurations (e.g., infinite wires, infinite sheets, long solenoids).

5. Common Mistakes

  • Negative Charge Trap: The Right-Hand Rule gives the force direction for a positive charge. If the particle is an electron, you MUST reverse the final direction!
  • Solenoid "n" Confusion: In B = μ0nI, n is NOT the total number of turns. It is the number of turns per meter (n = N/L).
  • Using Total Velocity for Radius: For helical motion, students often put total v into R = mv/qB. Wrong! Only the perpendicular component (v sinθ) dictates the radius.
  • Ignoring Vector Signs: When evaluating v × B using determinant form, basic sign errors in cross products cost easy marks.

6. Example Applications

Velocity Selector

A region has crossed E and B fields (E ⊥ B). A charge passes straight through undeflected if the electric and magnetic forces perfectly cancel out:

qE = qvB  &implies;  v = E/B

Comparing Particles

"A proton and an alpha particle are accelerated through the same potential V. Find the ratio of their radii."

Insight: Use R = (1/B)√(2mV/q). Since V and B are constant, R ∝ √(m/q).

For an alpha particle: mα = 4mp, qα = 2qp. So the ratio is 1 : √2.

7. IAT Exam Focus Points

Moving Coil Galvanometer (MCG):

Principle: A current-carrying coil placed in a uniform magnetic field experiences a torque (τ = NIAB sinθ). In a radial field, sinθ = 1, so τ = NIAB.

This torque is balanced by a restoring torque (τ = kφ) from the spring.

φ = (NAB/k)I
(k: Torsional constant, φ: Deflection)
Current Sensitivity

Is = φ/I = NAB/k

Voltage Sensitivity

Vs = φ/V = NAB/kR

Galvanometer Conversions (Very High Yield):

Target Component Formula
Ammeter Shunt (Small S) in Parallel S = IgRg / (I - Ig)
Voltmeter Multiplier (Large R) in Series R = (V / Ig) - Rg

Trajectory Proportionalities:

Master the relations for radius R in terms of Momentum (p), Kinetic Energy (K), and Potential (V). IAT loves asking to compare R for protons, deuterons, and alpha particles under these specific constraints.

Biot-Savart Superposition:

Expect questions with a wire bent into straight segments and semicircles. Add the magnetic fields vectorially using the right-hand thumb rule. Remember that straight segments pointing directly towards or away from the observation point contribute zero magnetic field.

Magnetic Moment Energy:

Treating a current coil as a magnetic dipole (m = NIA) and finding the work done to rotate it: W = mB(cosθ1 - cosθ2) is a classic crossover concept from electrostatics (electric dipoles).
