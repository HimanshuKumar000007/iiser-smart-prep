System of Particles and Rotational Motion

Hot Topic

1. Core Concept

Centre of Mass (COM)

A unique mathematical point where the entire mass of the system can be assumed to be concentrated for studying its translational motion.

The Rotational Analogy

Every translational concept has a rotational twin:

  • Mass (m) ↔ Moment of Inertia (I)
  • Force (F) ↔ Torque (τ)
  • Momentum (p) ↔ Angular Momentum (L)
  • Velocity (v) ↔ Angular Velocity (ω)

Rigid Body & Equilibrium

Rigid Body: A system of particles where the distance between any two points remains strictly constant. It cannot be deformed.

Equilibrium: A rigid body is in complete equilibrium ONLY if:
ΣFext = 0 (Translational) AND Στext = 0 (Rotational).

2. Key Formulas

Centre of Mass:

rcom = Σmiri / Σmi = ∫r dm / ∫dm
Position of the Centre of Mass.
vcom = Σmivi / Mtotal   |   acom = Fnet,ext / Mtotal
Motion of Centre of Mass.

Vectors & Relations:

A × B = (AB sinθ)n̂
Vector (Cross) Product. Direction given by Right-Hand Rule.
v = ω × r
Angular & Linear Velocity Relation.
a = α × r + ω × v
Acceleration (Tangential + Centripetal).

Rotational Dynamics:

I = Σmiri2 = ∫r2 dm
Moment of Inertia (MOI)
  • Torque (about a point): τ = r × F
  • Angular Momentum (Particle): L = r × p
  • Angular Momentum (Rigid Body, Fixed Axis): L = Iω
  • Newton's 2nd Law (Rotational): τnet = dL/dt = Iα
ω = ω0 + αt  |  θ = ω0t + (1/2)αt2  |  ω2 = ω02 + 2αθ
Rotational Kinematics (Constant α)

4. Conceptual Insights

Internal Forces do NO work on COM:

Internal explosions or spring forces can change the kinetic energy of the fragments, but they CANNOT change the velocity of the COM (acom = 0).

Torque is Origin-Dependent

The value of τ = r × F changes if you change your reference point (origin).
Exception: If the net force on a body is zero (a "couple"), the torque is the SAME about any point!

Conservation of Angular Momentum:

If τnet = 0, then L = constant.
For a rigid body: I1ω1 = I2ω2.
(e.g., Ice skater pulls arms in &implies; I decreases &implies; ω increases).

5. Common Traps & Mistakes

⚠️

Cross Product Order Reversal:

Writing v = r × ω instead of v = ω × r. TRAP! A × B = -(B × A). The order is strictly fixed.

⚠️

Misusing Parallel Axis Theorem:

Inew = I0 + Md2 ONLY works if I0 is the axis passing exactly through the Centre of Mass. You cannot use it to jump between two random parallel axes.

⚠️

Assuming COM is always inside the body:

The COM of a ring, a hollow sphere, or a boomerang lies in empty space outside the actual material.

⚠️

Translational vs Rotational K.E.:

Ktotal = Ktrans + Krot = (1/2)Mvcom2 + (1/2)Icomω2. Do not forget the rotational part for rolling objects!

6. Example Applications

Man walking on a boat (No external friction):

A man of mass m walks a distance L on a boat of mass M. How much does the boat shift?

Insight: Net external force is zero, so COM doesn't move (Δxcom = 0). Use formula: Boat Shift X = mL / (m + M).

Toppling of a Block:

A force F pushes a block of height h and base b. When will it topple?

Insight: Toppling occurs when the Normal reaction (N) shifts completely to the edge. Equate torques about that edge: F × h = mg × (b/2).

7. IAT Exam Focus Points

Conservation of L in Collisions (Crucial)

IAT loves "bullet hits a hinged rod" problems.

Method: Linear momentum is NOT conserved (hinge applies unknown force). Apply Conservation of Angular Momentum strictly about the hinge.
(Linitial_bullet = Lfinal_system &implies; mvu · r⊥ = Isystemω).

Rolling Motion Kinematics:

Pure rolling means the lowest point has zero velocity relative to the ground. &implies; vcom = Rω. Static friction does NO work in pure rolling.

Pure Rolling vs. Sliding Comparison

Feature Pure Rolling Sliding (on Smooth Surface)
Bottom Point Velocity vp = 0 vp = vcom
Work by Friction Zero (Static Friction) Negative (Kinetic Friction)
K.E. Composition Trans + Rotational Translational Only
K = (1/2)Mv2[1 + k2/R2]
Kinetic Energy of Rolling, where k is the radius of gyration (I = Mk2).

Trick: For Solid Sphere, k2/R2 = 2/5. For Disc, 1/2. For Ring, 1.

a = g sinθ /[1 + k2/R2]
Acceleration when rolling down an incline.

Pro Tip: The solid sphere always wins the race to the bottom because it has the smallest k2/R2, hence highest acceleration a.

Rolling Race Ranking

For same M and R, objects rolling down an incline rank by time taken (t):

tSolid Sphere < tSolid Disc < tHollow Sphere < tRing

Logic: More mass near center → Lower MOI → Lower k2/R2 → Higher Accel!
