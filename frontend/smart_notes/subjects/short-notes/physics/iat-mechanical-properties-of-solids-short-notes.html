Mechanical Properties of Solids (Elasticity)

Physics Unit 8
25 min read
IAT Advanced
Hot Topic

1. Core Concept

Elasticity

The property by which a body regains its original dimensions after the deforming force is removed. No material is perfectly elastic or perfectly plastic.

Stress & Strain

  • Stress (σ): The internal restoring force developed per unit area (σ = Frestore/A).
    Crucial Note: In equilibrium, Frestore = Fapplied. But if the body is accelerating, they are NOT equal!
  • Strain (ε): The fractional change in dimension. It is dimensionless and unitless. (ε = ΔL/L or ΔV/V).

Types of Stress & Strain Classification

Deforming Force Type of Stress Type of Strain Modulus
Perpendicular to Area Longitudinal / Normal Longitudinal (ΔL/L) Young's Modulus (Y)
Tangential to Area Shear / Tangential Shear (θ ≈ Δx/L) Rigidity Modulus (η)
Uniform Pressure Bulk / Hydraulic Volumetric (ΔV/V) Bulk Modulus (B)

2. Key Formulas & Moduli

Hooke’s Law

Within the proportional limit, Stress ∝ Strain. The proportionality constant is the Modulus of Elasticity.

📏

Young’s Modulus (Y)

What does it measure?

Tap to reveal

Y = (F/A) / (ΔL/L)
Measures resistance to longitudinal deformation (length change).

🧊

Bulk Modulus (B)

Definition of Compressibility?

Tap to reveal

B = - ΔP / (ΔV/V)
Measures resistance to volume change. C = 1/B.

📐

Shear Modulus (η)

Angular deformation?

Tap to reveal

η = (F/A) / θ
Measures resistance to shape change without volume change.

The Spring Analogy

Any uniform wire can be treated as a spring with stiffness k = YA/L. Hooke's law F = kΔL becomes exactly F = (YA/L)ΔL.

ν = -(Δr/r) / (ΔL/L)
Poisson’s Ratio (ν): Ratio of lateral strain to longitudinal strain.

3. Elite Shortcuts & Memory Tricks

Elongation due to Self-Weight

For a heavy uniform rod (mass M, length L) hanging vertically:

ΔL = MgL / (2AY) = ρgL2 / (2Y)

Pro Tip: It elongates exactly half as much as it would if the entire weight was hung at the bottom!

ΔV/V = (ΔL/L)(1 - 2ν)
Volume Change Trick: Volume is conserved only if ν = 0.5 (ideal rubber).

Thermal Stress:

  • Thermal Strain = αΔT
  • Thermal Force = YAαΔT (Crucial Shortcut: Completely independent of length!)

4. Conceptual Insights & Curves

The Stress-Strain Curve:

  • Slope = Y: Steeper slope means higher Y (Steel is more elastic than Rubber).
  • Ductile (Steel): Large plastic region, can be drawn into wires.
  • Brittle (Glass): Fractures suddenly near yield point.
  • Elastomers (Rubber): No straight-line (Hooke's) region, no plastic region.

Area Under Curve:

Area under Stress-Strain graph = Strain Energy Density (u = σ2/2Y).

Area under Force-Extension graph = Total Strain Energy.

5. Common Traps & Mistakes

⚠️

Thermal Expansion Trap:

If a rod is heated and free to expand, Thermal Stress is ZERO. Stress only develops if restricted.

⚠️

Breaking Force Trap:

Breaking Stress is a constant material property. Breaking Force increases with area (F ∝ r2).

⚠️

Sudden Loading:

Gradual load M gives extension Mg/k. Sudden load M gives DOUBLE extension 2Mg/k.

6. Example Applications

Composite Rods:

  • Series (End-to-End): Tension is same. keq = (k1k2)/(k1+k2).
  • Parallel (Side-by-Side): Strain is same. keq = k1+k2.

7. IAT Exam Focus Points

Bending of Beams:

Depression δ = WL3 / (4Ybd3). To minimize bending, increase depth (d) as it is cubed.

L0 = (L1T2 - L2T1) / (T2 - T1)
Finding natural length L0 from two different tension/length points.

Isothermal vs Adiabatic Bulk Modulus

Isothermal: Biso = P. Adiabatic: Badia = γP (Used for sound wave propagation).
