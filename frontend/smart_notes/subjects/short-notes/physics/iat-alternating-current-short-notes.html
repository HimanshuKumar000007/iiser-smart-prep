Alternating Current (AC)

Unit 7
25 min read
IAT Advanced

1. AC Basics & RMS Values

Alternating Current (AC) changes its magnitude and direction periodically. In India, the standard frequency is 50 Hz.

v = v₀ sin(ωt)  |  i = i₀ sin(ωt)
Instantaneous voltage and current. (ω = 2πf)
I_rms = i₀ / √2 ≈ 0.707 i₀
Root Mean Square Value. (The value shown by AC ammeters/voltmeters).
I_avg = 2i₀ / π ≈ 0.637 i₀
Average value over a half cycle. (Avg over a full cycle is zero).

2. Phasors (Rotating Vectors)

A Phasor is a vector that rotates around the origin with angular speed ω. The vertical projection represents the instantaneous value.

  • Phasor Diagram: Used to visualize the phase relationship between voltage and current.
  • In Phase: Vectors point in the same direction.
  • Out of Phase: Vectors separated by an angle φ.

3. Pure AC Circuits

A. Purely Resistor (R)

Voltage and Current are in phase (φ = 0°).

i = (v₀ / R) sin(ωt)
Ohm's law applies directly.

B. Purely Inductor (L)

Voltage leads Current by 90° (π/2 radians).

X_L = ωL = 2πfL
Inductive Reactance (Measured in Ohms). Increases with frequency.

C. Purely Capacitor (C)

Current leads Voltage by 90° (π/2 radians).

X_C = 1 / (ωC) = 1 / (2πfC)
Capacitive Reactance (Measured in Ohms). Decreases with frequency.

4. Series LCR Circuit & Resonance

In a series LCR circuit, we combine the impedances using vector addition (Phasors).

Z = √[R² + (X_L - X_C)²]
Impedance (Z): Total opposition to current.
tan φ = (X_L - X_C) / R
Phase constant between voltage and current.

Electrical Resonance:

Occurs when X_L = X_C. At this point, the circuit becomes purely resistive.

  • Resonant Frequency: f₀ = 1 / [2π √(LC)]
  • Impedance is Minimum: Z = R
  • Current is Maximum: i = v / R
Q = (1/R) √(L/C) = ω₀L / R
Quality Factor (Q): Sharpness of resonance. Higher Q means better selectivity.

5. Power in AC Circuit

Pure inductors and capacitors consume zero average power because current and voltage are 90° apart.

P_avg = V_rms I_rms cos φ
Average Power. (cos φ is the Power Factor).
cos φ = R / Z
Power Factor. Pure R: cos φ = 1. Pure L/C: cos φ = 0.

Wattless Current

The component of current (I sin φ) that does not consume any power is called Wattless Current.

6. Transformers

Used to increase or decrease AC voltage based on Mutual Induction.

V_s / V_p = N_s / N_p = I_p / I_s = k
k = Transformation Ratio. (k > 1 for Step-up).
η = (Output Power / Input Power) × 100%
Efficiency. (Ideal transformer is 100%).

Energy Losses in Transformers:

  • Flux Leakage: Not all primary flux reaches secondary.
  • Resistance Loss (Copper Loss): Heat in windings (I²R).
  • Eddy Current Loss (Iron Loss): Minimized by laminating the core.
  • Hysteresis Loss: Energy lost in reversing magnetization.
