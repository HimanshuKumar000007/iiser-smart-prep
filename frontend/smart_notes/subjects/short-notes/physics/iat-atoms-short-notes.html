1. Core Concept & Atomic Models

The atom has evolved from a solid sphere to a complex quantum system. The IAT focuses on the transition from classical to quantized models.

Rutherford Model

  • Nuclear Atom: Mass and (+) charge in a tiny nucleus.
  • Classical Orbits: Electrons revolve like planets.
  • Failure: Cannot explain stability or discrete line spectra.

Bohr Model

  • Quantized Orbits: Stable orbits with fixed energy levels.
  • Quantum Jumps: Energy emitted/absorbed as photons.
  • Success: Explains Hydrogen stability and spectra perfectly.

2. Alpha-Particle Scattering

Geiger-Marsden experiment results that led to the discovery of the nucleus.

Key Observations

  • Most particles: Passed straight through (Atoms are 99.9% empty).
  • Some particles: Deflected by small angles.
  • 1 in 8000: Rebounded (θ > 90°) - discovered the dense (+) nucleus.
r0 = (1/4πε0) × (2Ze2 / K)
Distance of Closest Approach. Head-on collision (b=0). PE = KE at this point.
b = [Ze2 cot(θ/2)] / [4πε0K]
Impact Parameter (b). Perpendicular distance from nuclear axis.

3. Bohr's Postulates & Key Formulas

Bohr's Three Postulates

  1. Stable Orbits: Electrons revolve in certain stable non-radiating orbits (Stationary states).
  2. Quantization: Angular momentum is an integer multiple of h/2π &implies; mvr = nh/2π.
  3. Frequency Condition: Photon energy equals the difference in energy levels &implies; hν = Ei - Ef.
rn = 0.529 × (n2 / Z) Å
Radius: &implies; r ∝ n2 / Z
vn = 2.18 × 106 × (Z / n) m/s
Velocity: &implies; v ∝ Z / n
En = -13.6 × (Z2 / n2) eV
Total Energy: &implies; E ∝ Z2 / n2
TE = -KE = PE / 2
Energy Relations (Shortcut): Crucial for solving fast.
1/λ = RZ2 (1/n12 - 1/n22)
Rydberg Formula: (R ≈ 1.097 × 107 m-1).

4. Spectral Series of Hydrogen

When an electron jumps from a higher orbit (n2) to a lower orbit (n1), it emits a photon. The collection of these wavelengths forms the spectral series.

Series n1 (Final) n2 (Initial) Region
Lyman 1 2, 3, 4 ... ∞ Ultraviolet
Balmer 2 3, 4, 5 ... ∞ Visible
Paschen 3 4, 5, 6 ... ∞ Infrared
Brackett/Pfund 4 / 5 Next ... ∞ Far Infrared
N = [n(n-1)] / 2
Total spectral lines emitted when electron falls from state n to ground state.

5. De Broglie’s Explanation

Standing Wave Condition

Bohr's 2nd postulate (mvr = nh/2π) was purely empirical until De Broglie explained it using matter waves. He proposed that an electron in a stable orbit forms a standing wave.

  • Condition for a stable loop: Circumference = n × wavelength
  • &implies; 2πr = nλ
  • Substitute λ = h/mv (De Broglie relation):
  • 2πr = n(h/mv) &implies; mvr = nh/2π

This proved that only those orbits are allowed where the electron wave constructive interferes with itself.

6. Conceptual Insights

Impact Parameter (b)

The perpendicular distance of the incoming α-particle's initial velocity vector from the nucleus's central axis. A smaller b causes a larger scattering angle θ. If b = 0 (a direct head-on collision), the particle rebounds entirely (θ = 180°).

Meaning of Negative Energy

The total energy En is negative because the electron is permanently bound to the nucleus (it sits in a potential well). If the total energy ever becomes ≥ 0, the electron is completely freed from the nucleus (ionized).

Number of Waves

The number of full de Broglie waves that mathematically fit into the nth orbit's circumference is exactly equal to n.

7. Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting Z2: Applying standard Hydrogen formulas (where Z = 1) directly to He+ (where Z = 2) or Li2+ (where Z = 3) without modifying the equations. Always keep the Z factor in your formulas!
  • "Longest" vs "Shortest" Wavelength:
    Longest Wavelength = Minimum Energy = The shortest possible jump (e.g., n = 3 → 2 for the Balmer series).
    Shortest Wavelength (Series Limit) = Maximum Energy = Jump from absolute infinity (e.g., n = ∞ → 2 for the Balmer series).
  • Excited State vs Quantum Number: "1st excited state" strictly means n = 2 (not n = 1, which is the ground state). "2nd excited state" means n = 3, and so on.

8. Example Applications

Proportionality Trap

"What is the ratio of the Time Period of an electron in the 2nd orbit of He+ to the 3rd orbit of Li2+?"

Insight: The Time period T = 2πr / v. Since r ∝ n2 / Z and v ∝ Z / n, we get T ∝ n3 / Z2.
Just plug in the values (He+: n=2, Z=2 and Li2+: n=3, Z=3) and solve it in 10 seconds!

9. IAT Exam Focus Points

Memorize Hydrogen Energy Levels

Do not waste time calculating these in the exam!

  • n = 1 → -13.6 eV
  • n = 2 → -3.4 eV (Gap: 10.2 eV)
  • n = 3 → -1.51 eV (Gap from n=1: 12.09 eV)
  • n = 4 → -0.85 eV (Gap from n=1: 12.75 eV)

If an IAT question says "a photon of exactly 12.09 eV excites an H-atom," you should instantly know it jumped precisely to n = 3.

KE / PE / TE Relations:

A very common question format. Remember: TE = -KE = PE / 2. If the Total Energy is given as -3.4 eV, the Kinetic Energy is automatically +3.4 eV and the Potential Energy is -6.8 eV.

Recoil of Atom:

Sometimes tied with momentum conservation problems. When a photon of energy E is emitted, the remaining atom physically recoils backward with a momentum equal to p = E/c.

Alpha Scattering Graph:

Be familiar with the graph of the number of scattered particles N(θ) vs the scattering angle θ. It drops off extremely rapidly because N ∝ 1 / sin4(θ/2).

10. Quick Check Quiz

Conceptual

Q1: An electron makes a transition from n=4 to n=2. Which spectral series does this photon belong to?

Correct! Since the final state is n=2, the transition belongs to the Balmer series (Visible region).
Proportionality

Q2: If the radius of the 1st Bohr orbit is a0, what is the radius of the 3rd orbit of the same atom?

Correct! Radius r ∝ n2. For n=3, the radius becomes 32 = 9 times the initial radius.
