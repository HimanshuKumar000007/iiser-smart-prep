Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter

Physics Unit 11
20 min read
IAT Advanced
Hot Topic

1. Core Concept & Electron Emission

Light propagates as a wave but interacts with matter as a stream of particles (photons). Conversely, moving material particles (like electrons) exhibit wave-like properties.

Thermionic

Emission by Heating a metal.

Field

Emission by Strong Electric Fields.

Photoelectric

Emission by Light of ν > ν0.

Work Function (Φ0)

Minimum energy required to eject an electron. 1 eV = 1.6 × 10-19 J. Lowest for Cesium (2.14 eV), highest for Platinum (5.65 eV).

2. Experimental Study

Observations from Hallwach's and Lenard's experiment:

Effect of Intensity

Photoelectric current is linearly proportional to light intensity. (More photons = More electrons).

Effect of Frequency

Stopping potential and Kmax increase linearly with frequency. Below threshold frequency (ν0), no emission occurs.

Saturation Current

The maximum current reached when all emitted electrons reach the collector. It depends only on intensity, not frequency.

4. Important Results

Wave Theory Failure vs. Reality

Feature Wave Theory Predicts Experimental Observation
Energy vs Intensity Kmax should increase with intensity. Kmax is independent of intensity.
Time Delay Lag for energy accumulation (~hours). Instantaneous emission (< 10-9 s).
Threshold ν Emission possible at any ν with high intensity. No emission below ν0, regardless of intensity.
  • 1-to-1 Interaction: One photon interacts with exactly one electron.
  • Saturation Current: Proportional to intensity (number of photons).

5. Particle Nature of Light: The Photon

Einstein proposed that radiation travels in discrete packets of energy called quanta or photons.

  • Energy: E = hν = hc/λ
  • Speed: Moves with speed of light (c) in vacuum.
  • Rest Mass: Photon has zero rest mass.
  • Momentum: p = E/c = h/λ.
  • Neutrality: Photons are electrically neutral (not deflected by E or B fields).
  • Collisions: Total energy/momentum conserved; photon number may change.

6. Wave Nature of Matter

De Broglie proposed that material particles like electrons also exhibit wave properties.

Davisson-Germer Experiment: Confirmed the wave nature of electrons by observing diffraction effects when an electron beam hit a Nickel crystal. The experimental wavelength matched the de Broglie prediction.

7. Conceptual Insights

Why Wave Theory Failed:

Classical wave theory predicted that (1) intense low-frequency light should eventually build up enough energy to eject electrons, and (2) dim light would cause a time delay in emission while energy accumulated. Experiments proved both predictions spectacularly wrong.

Not all electrons get Kmax:

Einstein's equation strictly gives Kmax. Electrons deeper inside the metal lose energy to collisions on their way out. Thus, emitted electrons have kinetic energies ranging from 0 to Kmax.

Visual Intuition for Stopping Potential:

Imagine electrons rolling up a hill of height V0. Only the fastest ones (with Kmax) make it to the top. If we raise the hill (increase opposing voltage) until even the fastest one turns back just before the peak, we have successfully found the stopping potential.

8. Common Mistakes

  • Unit Mismatch: Mixing Joules and electron-volts (eV). Always convert carefully: 1 eV = 1.6 × 10-19 J.
  • "Intensity" Trap: Assuming higher intensity means higher energy electrons. Wrong. Intensity only means more electrons are ejected per second, not faster electrons.
  • Slope Confusion: In a graph of Stopping Potential (V0) vs. frequency (ν), the slope is h/e, NOT just h. (Because V0 = (h/e)ν - (Φ0/e)).
  • De Broglie of Photons: Do not use λ = h/√(2mK) for photons! Photons have zero rest mass. Use λ = hc/E instead.

9. Example Applications

The Calculation Shortcut

Always use hc ≈ 1240 eV·nm (or 12400 eV·Å) for photon energy calculations.

Example: If light of λ = 310 nm hits a metal, the photon energy is E = 1240 / 310 = 4 eV. If the work function is 2.5 eV, then Kmax = 4 - 2.5 = 1.5 eV.

Comparing Particles

Find the ratio of de Broglie wavelengths for a proton and an alpha particle accelerated through the same potential V.

Insight: λ ∝ 1/√(mq). Since mα = 4mp and qα = 2qp, the product mq for an alpha particle is 8 times that of a proton. So, λp / λα = √8 / 1 = 2√2.

10. IAT Exam Focus Points

Graphs are Guaranteed:

Master the three main graphs:

  • Current vs. Voltage: Shows the effect of varying intensity and varying frequency.
  • Stopping Potential vs. Frequency: Parallel lines for different metals; the slope is the universal constant h/e; the x-intercept is ν0.
  • Kmax vs. Frequency: Similar to above, but slope is h.

Ratios of Wavelengths:

Expect problems asking you to strictly compare the λ of electrons, protons, and alpha particles given the same Momentum, same Kinetic Energy, or same Accelerating Potential. Know the proportionalities!

Photon Flux:

You might be asked to find the number of photons emitted per second by a light bulb of Power P. Formula: n = P / Ephoton = Pλ / (hc).

Stopping Potential Condition:

Remember that for the photoelectric current to be exactly zero, the reverse (negative) voltage must be applied to the anode (collector) relative to the cathode (emitter).

11. Practice & Assessment

Quick Check

Q1. In the photoelectric effect, if the intensity of incident light is doubled, the stopping potential will:

Correct! Stopping potential depends only on the frequency of light and the nature of the metal, not its intensity.

Q2. A proton and an alpha particle have the same kinetic energy. What is the ratio λp / λα?

Correct! λ = h/√(2mK). Since K is same, λ ∝ 1/√m. Since mα = 4mp, the ratio is √4 : 1 = 2 : 1.
