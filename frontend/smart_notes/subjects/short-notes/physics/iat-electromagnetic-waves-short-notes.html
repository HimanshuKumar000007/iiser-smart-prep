Electromagnetic Waves

Physics Unit 8
15 min read
IAT Advanced
Hot Topic

1. Core Concept

Accelerating electric charges produce time-varying electric (E) and magnetic (B) fields. A changing E field creates a changing B field, and vice-versa. These fields continuously regenerate each other, propagating through space as an Electromagnetic (EM) wave. No material medium is required.

2. The Electromagnetic Spectrum

The EM spectrum classifies waves by frequency/wavelength. Below is the full spectrum in increasing frequency order:

Wave Type Production Primary Use
Radio Waves Accelerated charges in wires AM/FM radio, cellular comm.
Microwaves Klystron/Magnetron valves Radar, microwave ovens
Infrared Vibration of atoms/molecules Remote controls, night vision
Visible Light Electron transitions in atoms Human vision, photography
Ultraviolet Inner shell electron jumps Sterilization, water purifiers
X-Rays Bombarding metal targets Medical imaging, security
Gamma Rays Radioactive nuclei decay Cancer therapy, nuclear physics

3. Key Formulas

Id = ε0 (dΦE/dt)
Displacement Current. (Current arising due to changing electric flux).
∮ B · dl = μ0(Ic + Id)
Ampere-Maxwell Law. Generalizes Ampere's law.

Wave Equations:

E = E0 sin(kx - ωt) ĵ
B = B0 sin(kx - ωt) k̂
Standard equations for EM waves propagating in the +x direction.

Wave Speed (c):

c = ω/k = E0/B0 = 1/√(μ0ε0)
Speed of light in a vacuum.

Energy Density (u):

uavg = ε0Erms2 = ½ε0E02
Crucial result: Average Energy Density. Shared 50/50 between E and B fields.

4. Important Results

  • Direction of Propagation: Strictly travels in the direction of E × B.
  • Phase Sync: E and B oscillate perfectly in phase.
  • Transverse Nature: Both fields are perpendicular to each other and the propagation direction.

5. Conceptual Insights

Why Displacement Current?

Maxwell realized that Ampere's original law failed between the plates of a charging capacitor. Id proves that a changing electric field itself generates a magnetic field.

6. Common Mistakes

  • Vector Direction: Use the right-hand rule for E × B = v. If E is [j] and wave is [i], B must be [k].
  • Energy Distribution: Even though E0 ≫ B0, the energy is shared exactly 50/50.

7. Example Applications

Radiation Pressure

Perfectly Absorbing: P = I/c. Perfectly Reflecting: P = 2I/c. Always check if the surface is a mirror!
