1. Core Concept

Electrostatics Basics

Electric charge is an intrinsic property of matter. Charges are quantized, conserved, and additive.

Property Conductors Insulators (Dielectrics)
Charge Mobility Free to move (Valence electrons) Fixed in position
Internal Field E = 0 (in static equilibrium) E ≠ 0 (can be polarized)
Charge Location Resides strictly on outer surface Distributed throughout volume
q = ±ne
Quantization of Charge. e ≈ 1.6 × 10-19 C.

2. Key Laws & Fields

F = (1/4πε0) · |q1q2|/r2
Coulomb’s Law. ε0 ≈ 8.854 × 10-12 C2/N·m2.
E = F/q0 = (1/4πε0) · q/r2
Electric Field Strength (E) for a point charge.
Φ = ∮ E · dA = qin/ε0
Gauss’s Law. Flux (Φ) depends only on enclosed charge.

4. Gauss's Law Applications

Distribution Case Electric Field (E)
Infinite Wire Distance r λ / (2πε0r)

Spherical Shell Inside (r < R) 0
Spherical Shell Outside (r ≥ R) Q / (4πε0r2)

Flux Logic

Flux through a closed surface depends only on qin. Doubling surface radius does not change the flux.

6. Common Traps & Mistakes

Vector Addition Error

E and F are vectors! You must resolve components (Ex, Ey) before adding. Never add magnitudes directly unless parallel.

Flux through a Surface

In Φ = ∮ E · dA, E is the total field (all charges), but qin is ONLY the charge inside. External charges affect E but contribute zero net flux.

7. Example Applications

Equilibrium of Charges

For three charges to be in equilibrium, the net force on each charge must be zero. The middle charge must be of opposite sign to the outer two.

Flux through Geometric Shapes

  • Cube (Charge at center): Φface = q / (6ε0)
  • Cube (Charge at corner): Φtotal = q / (8ε0)

8. IAT Exam Focus Points

E-Field Proportionality

Point Charge: E ∝ 1/r2 | Dipole: E ∝ 1/r3 | Wire: E ∝ 1/r | Sheet: E ∝ r0 (Constant).

High Yield: Conductors

Charges always reside on the outer skin. The surface is an equipotential; field lines are ALWAYS perpendicular to the surface.
