Ray Optics & Optical Instruments

Physics Unit 9
25 min read
IAT Advanced
Hot Topic

1. Core Concept

Light travels in straight lines (rays) in a homogeneous medium. When it encounters a boundary, it can bounce back (Reflection) or bend into the new medium (Refraction). All image formation relies on the intersection (real) or apparent intersection (virtual) of these rays.

2. Reflection by Spherical Mirrors

Reflection occurs when light bounces off a polished surface. For spherical mirrors (concave and convex), the focal length f = R/2.

1/v + 1/u = 1/f
Mirror Formula. u = object distance, v = image distance, f = focal length.
m = hi/ho = -v/u
Magnification. |m| > 1 means enlarged, |m| < 1 means diminished.

Sign Convention: Incident light is +ve. Pole is origin. Object is always placed on the left (-u).

3. Refraction

Refraction is the bending of light when transitioning from one medium to another due to change in speed.

n1 sin(i) = n2 sin(r)
Snell’s Law. n = refractive index = c/v.
n21 = v1/v2 = λ1/λ2
Refractive index of medium 2 w.r.t 1. Frequency remains constant!

4. Total Internal Reflection (TIR)

TIR occurs when light travels from a denser to a rarer medium at an angle greater than the critical angle.

sin(C) = 1 / n
Critical Angle (C). n is the refractive index of denser w.r.t rarer.

Applications of TIR

Optical fibers, mirages, and the brilliance of diamonds are all due to multiple TIRs.

5. Refraction at Spherical Surfaces and Lenses

n2/v - n1/u = (n2 - n1)/R
Refraction at Single Spherical Surface.
1/f = (n - 1)(1/R1 - 1/R2)
Lens Maker’s Formula. Correct for thin lenses.
1/v - 1/u = 1/f
Thin Lens Formula. Note the minus sign (-).

Lens Power & Combination

Power P = 1/f (m). For lenses in contact: Peq = P1 + P2 ... and 1/feq = 1/f1 + 1/f2 ...

6. Refraction through a Prism

A prism deviates light. The angle of deviation (δ) depends on the angle of incidence.

A + δ = i + e
Relation between prism angle (A), deviation (δ), incidence (i), and emergence (e).
n = sin((A + δm)/2) / sin(A/2)
Prism Formula. δm is the minimum deviation.

7. Optical Instruments

Simple Microscope

A single convex lens. Magnifying Power M = 1 + D/f (image at D) or M = D/f (image at infinity).

Compound Microscope

Uses objective and eyepiece. Total magnification m = mo × me.

Near point: M = (L/fo)(1 + D/fe).

Astronomical Telescope

Normal adjustment (infinity): M = fo / fe and Tube Length L = fo + fe.
