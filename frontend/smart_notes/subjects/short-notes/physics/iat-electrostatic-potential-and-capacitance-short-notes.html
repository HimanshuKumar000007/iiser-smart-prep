1. Electrostatic Potential

Fundamental Concepts

Electric potential (V) is the work done per unit positive charge in bringing it from infinity to a point. It is a scalar field.

Feature Electric Potential (V) Potential Energy (U)
Nature Property of a point Property of a system
Formula V = kq/r U = kq1q2/r
Unit Volt (J/C) Joule (J)
V = W / q0
Potential (V) is the work done (W) per test charge (q0).
E = -dV/dr
Potential Gradient. Field (E) points towards decreasing potential.

2. Electric Dipole Potential

V = (k · p cosθ) / r2
Potential due to dipole at distance r and angle θ.
  • Axial (θ = 0°): Vmax = kp/r2
  • Equatorial (θ = 90°): V = 0 (Zero potential plane)

Proportionality: For a point charge V ∝ 1/r, but for a dipole V ∝ 1/r2.

3. System of Charges

Scalar Nature: Potential follows the principle of superposition. Simply add potentials algebraically: Vnet = Σ Vi.

Common IAT Trap: V = 0 at a point does not mean E = 0. Example: Center of a dipole.

4. Equipotential Surfaces

  • Work Done: W = qΔV = 0 (Moving charge along the surface).
  • Direction: E-Field Lines are always perpendicular to equipotential surfaces.
  • Spacing: Surfaces are closer where E is stronger (d = |dV/E|).

5. Potential Energy

U = k · q1q2 / r
System of two point charges. Energy is Negative for attraction.
U = -p · E cosθ
Potential Energy of a dipole in external field. Stable at θ = 0°.

8. Electrostatics of Conductors

  • Static E: Field is zero inside the conductor.
  • Potential: Constantly equal throughout the volume.
  • Charge: Resides entirely on the outer skin.
  • Shielding: Cavity in a conductor has zero field regardless of external charges.
