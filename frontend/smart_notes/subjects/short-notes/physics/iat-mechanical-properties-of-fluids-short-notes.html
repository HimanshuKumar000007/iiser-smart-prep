1. Core Concept

Fluid Statics vs. Fluid Dynamics

Feature Fluid Statics Fluid Dynamics
Condition Fluid at rest (v = 0) Fluid in motion (v &neq; 0)
Focus Pressure, Buoyancy, Density Flow rates, Velocity, Energy
Core Law Pascal's Law / Archimedes Bernoulli's / Equation of Continuity

Surface Tension & Viscosity

  • Surface Tension (S or T): The free surface of a liquid behaves like a stretched elastic membrane trying to minimize its surface area due to unbalanced cohesive forces acting on surface molecules.
  • Viscosity (η): The "internal friction" of a fluid that opposes the relative motion between its adjacent layers.

2. Key Formulas

Pressure & Buoyancy:

P = Patm + ρgh
Absolute Pressure.
FB = Vsubmerged × ρliquid × g
Buoyant Force (Archimedes' Principle).

Fluid Dynamics:

A1v1 = A2v2
Equation of Continuity (Conservation of Mass).
P + ρgh + (1/2)ρv2 = Constant
Bernoulli’s Principle (Conservation of Energy).

3. Elite Shortcuts & Memory Tricks

v = √(2gh)
Torricelli’s Law (Speed of Efflux at depth h).

Maximum Range of Efflux:

Range R = 2√(h(H-h)). Maximum range occurs when the hole is at midpoint h = H/2, and Rmax = H.

Terminal Velocity Merging Trick

If N identical small drops (each with terminal velocity v) merge into one single big drop:

Vnew = N2/3 × v

Example: 8 drops merge &implies; 82/3 = 4v. The big drop falls 4 times faster!

W = S × (2 × 4πR2) = 8πR2S
Work to blow a soap bubble (Factor of 2 for two surfaces!).

4. Conceptual Insights & Intuition

Bernoulli’s Intuition:

Where velocity is HIGH, pressure is LOW. (This explains airplane wing lift, roofs blowing off in storms, and cricket ball swing).

Angle of Contact (θ):

  • Acute (θ < 90°): Liquid "wets" solid (e.g., water in glass). Meniscus: Concave. Capillary rise.
  • Obtuse (θ > 90°): Liquid doesn't wet (e.g., mercury in glass). Meniscus: Convex. Capillary fall.

Streamline vs. Turbulent:

Streamlines never cross. If velocity > Critical Velocity (Reynolds Number NR > 2000), flow becomes chaotic (Turbulent).

5. Common Traps & Mistakes

⚠️

The Soap Bubble vs Liquid Drop Trap:

Soap bubble in air has TWO surfaces (inner and outer). A water drop in air, or air bubble in water, has ONE.

⚠️

Archimedes Density Trap:

Buoyant force FB = Vsub · ρliquid · g. It depends on the density of the liquid, not the object.

⚠️

Bernoulli for Viscous Fluids:

Bernoulli strictly assumes zero viscosity. If the fluid is viscous (like honey), energy is lost as heat, so the Bernoulli constant drops along the flow.

6. Example Applications

Ice Cube Melting:

An ice cube floating in water melts. Does the water level rise, fall, or stay the same?

Insight: Stays the same! The floating ice displaces exactly its own weight in water. (Unless the ice contains a heavy coin—then the level falls).

Venturimeter:

Constriction in pipe reduces Area &implies; increases velocity &implies; decreases pressure (Bernoulli). Measuring this ΔP gives flow speed!

7. IAT Exam Focus Points

Accelerating Containers:

Liquid surface tilts by tanθ = a/g. Effective gravity becomes geff = √(g2 + a2).

Capillary Tube of Insufficient Length:

If tube length L < h, the liquid does not overflow. The meniscus flattens (radius increases) to maintain equilibrium: h × R = Constant.

Thermodynamics Link

Splitting Drops: Increases total surface area &implies; energy supplied &implies; liquid cools.

Merging Drops: Decreases total surface area &implies; surface energy released &implies; liquid heats up.
