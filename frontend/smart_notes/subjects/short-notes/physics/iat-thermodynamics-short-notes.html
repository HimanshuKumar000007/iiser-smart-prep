Hot Topic

1. Core Concept

Zeroth Law

Establishes Temperature (T) as a state variable for thermal equilibrium.

ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW
First Law of Thermodynamics: The ultimate energy balance.

State Variables

State Variables: Properties that depend only on the current state (P, V, T, U, S). Note: Work and Heat are NOT state variables—they are path-dependent.

Note: For ideal gases, ΔU = μCvΔT always, regardless of the process.

Molar Heat Capacities (Cp & Cv)

For an ideal gas, specific heat depends on the process:

  • Cv: Molar heat at constant volume. Cv = (f/2)R.
  • Cp: Molar heat at constant pressure. Cp = Cv + R = (1 + f/2)R.
  • Mayer's Relation: Cp - Cv = R.

2. Sign Convention (The "IAT Rule")

Master Your Signs

In ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW, the standard physics convention (used in IAT) is:

  • ΔQ: (+) when Heat enters | (-) when Heat leaves.
  • ΔW: (+) when Gas expands (Work by gas) | (-) when Gas compresses (Work on gas).
  • ΔU: (+) when Temp rises | (-) when Temp falls.

4. Elite Shortcuts & Tricks (Time Savers)

C = Cv + R / (1 - x)
Polytropic Process (PVx = constant) Molar Heat Capacity.

Adiabatic vs. Isothermal Slope:

For the same point (P,V) on a graph: (Slope)Adiabatic = γ × (Slope)Isothermal. Adiabatic is always steeper.

Carnot Engine vs. Refrigerator

Feature Carnot Engine Refrigerator (Fridge/AC)
Goal Convert Heat to Work Transfer Heat from Cold to Hot
Metric Efficiency (η) Coeff. of Performance (β or COP)
Formula η = 1 - TL/TH β = TL / (TH - TL)
Relation β = (1 - η) / η

Cyclic Efficiency:

Work done = Area of loop. Clockwise = Positive Work (Engine). Counter-clockwise = Negative Work (Refrigerator).

η = 1 - (TL / TH)
Carnot Limit: Maximum efficiency (Temperatures in Kelvin).

5. Conceptual Insights

Degrees of Freedom (f) & γ:

  • Monoatomic (He): f=3, γ=1.67
  • Diatomic (O2): f=5, γ=1.4
  • Polyatomic (CO2): f=6, γ=1.33
  • γ = 1 + 2/f

Internal Energy Pitfall:

For an ideal gas, ΔU depends ONLY on ΔT. In an isothermal process for an ideal gas, ΔU = 0, even if the gas expands significantly.

Second Law (Entropy & Direction)

Total entropy of an isolated system never decreases. Heat only flows spontaneously from hot to cold (Clausius statement). No engine can be 100% efficient (Kelvin-Planck statement).

Reversible vs. Irreversible Processes

Feature Reversible Irreversible
Description Can be retraced completely. Cannot be retraced.
Execution Extremely slow (Quasi-static). Rapid and spontaneous.
Dissipation No friction or viscosity. Involves friction, turbulence, etc.

6. Common Traps & Mistakes

⚠️

Sign Convention Inconsistency:

Always use: ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW. If work is done ON the gas, ΔW is negative. If heat is removed, ΔQ is negative.

⚠️

Kelvin Conversion:

Forgetting to add 273.15 to Celsius temperatures in Carnot or Isothermal problems is a common error.

⚠️

The "Slope" Confusion:

In a P-V diagram, be careful! The steepness relationship changes during compression vs expansion. Always rely on the γ factor.

7. Example Applications

Calculating Cv for a Mixture:

1 mole of He (Mono) + 1 mole of H2 (Dia). Find mixture Cv,mix.

Insight: Use mole-weighted average: Cv,mix = (μ1Cv1 + μ2Cv2) / (μ1 + μ2).

Reversible Engine Limits:

Engine between 300K and 600K. Max efficiency = 1 - (300/600) = 0.5. Any answer > 0.5 is physically impossible.

8. IAT Exam Focus Points

Graph Identification (P-V diagrams):

  • Vertical (V=const) = Isochoric
  • Horizontal (P=const) = Isobaric
  • Rectangular Hyperbola (PV=const) = Isothermal
  • Steeper than hyperbola = Adiabatic

Work in Cyclic Graphs

If IAT provides a triangle or circle in a P-V diagram, DO NOT integrate! Use Area = (1/2)bh (triangle) or Area = πr2 (circle).

Equipartition of Energy:

ΔU = (f/2)μRΔT. If unspecified, assume "Ideal Gas" (Monoatomic unless stated otherwise).
