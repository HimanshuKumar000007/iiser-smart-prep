Motion in a Plane (Vectors, Projectiles & Circular Motion)

Crucial Foundation

1. Core Concept

Independence of Axes (Galilean Principle)

2D motion is simply two independent 1D motions (x and y axes) happening simultaneously. The motion along the x-axis does not affect the motion along the y-axis, and vice versa. Their only shared variable is Time (t).

Scalars vs Vectors

Feature Scalars Vectors
Definition Magnitude only Magnitude + Direction
Examples Mass, Time, Speed Force, Velocity, Accel.
Addition Arithmetic ($3+4=7$) Vector Laws (Resultant)

Resolution of Vectors

Breaking a vector into mutually perpendicular components (Ax = A cos θ, Ay = A sin θ). This converts complex 2D vector geometry into simple 1D algebra.

Equations of Motion in 2D

When acceleration is constant, motion along perpendicular axes is independent:

v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2a·s

Tip: For projectiles, $a_x = 0$ and $a_y = -g$.

2. Key Formulas & Vectors

Vector Addition & Subtraction

Graphical Method: Use the "Tip-to-Tail" rule.
• Triangle Law: Join head of A to tail of B.
• Parallelogram Law: Join tails of both; resultant is the diagonal.

Analytical Method: Given angle θ between vectors A and B:

|A ± B| = √(A² + B² ± 2AB cos θ)
Magnitude of the Resultant Vector
tan α = B sin θ / (A + B cos θ)
Direction of Resultant (Angle α is with vector A)

Multiplication by Real Number (λ)

Multiplying vector A by λ changes its magnitude by $|λ|$.

  • λ > 0: Direction same as A.
  • λ < 0: Direction opposite to A.
  • λ = 0: Becomes a Null Vector ($\vec{0}$).

Unit Vectors ($\hat{i}, \hat{j}, \hat{k}$)

Vectors of magnitude 1 used to specify direction.
Formula: â = A / |A|

Magnitude of $\vec{A} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$:
|A| = √(x² + y² + z²)

Vector Products

  • Dot Product (Scalar): A · B = AB cos θ = AxBx + AyBy + AzBz. Used to find work, power, or check if vectors are perpendicular (⊥).
  • Cross Product (Vector): |A × B| = AB sin θ. Direction is given by the Right-Hand Rule. Represents the Area of a parallelogram formed by the two vectors.
vAB = vA − vB
Relative Velocity of A as observed by B

3. Projectile Motion Formulas

(Given initial velocity u, angle θ with horizontal, ax = 0, ay = −g)

Time of Flight (T) = 2u sin θ / g = 2uy / g
Total time spent in the air
Max Height (H) = u² sin²θ / 2g = uy² / 2g
Maximum vertical displacement
Horizontal Range (R) = u² sin(2θ) / g = 2uxuy / g
Total horizontal displacement

Projectile State at Time 't'

  • Velocity: v = (u cos θ)î + (u sin θ − gt)ȷ
  • Speed: v = √(u² + g²t² − 2ugt sin θ)
  • Angle with horizontal: tan φ = vy / vx
y = x tan θ − gx² / (2u² cos²θ) = x tan θ (1 − x/R)
Equation of Trajectory (Parabola)

Symmetry Principle

For ground-to-ground projectiles:

  • Velocity Symmetry: Speed at the same vertical height is identical during ascent and descent.
  • Angle Symmetry: The angle with the horizontal is $+\theta$ (pointing up) at some height during ascent, and $-\theta$ (pointing down) at the same height during descent.

4. Shortcuts & Relative Motion

Projectile Magic Relations

  • Universal Relation: R tan θ = 4H (Always true for any angle).
  • Complementary Angles (θ and 90° − θ): They yield the Same Range R.
    Relation with heights: R = 4√(H1H2).
    Relation with times: T1T2 = 2R / g.
  • Maximum Range: Rmax = u² / g (occurs at 45°). At this angle, H = Rmax / 4.
  • Average Velocity: For the entire flight, the average velocity is exactly u cos θ (horizontal), because the net vertical displacement is zero!
|Δv| = 2v sin(θ/2)
Circular Motion ΔV Trick: A particle moves in a circle with constant speed v. The magnitude of change in velocity after rotating by angle θ is given by this formula.

Relative Motion Models

Rain-Man Problems

To find the angle to hold the umbrella, find vrm (velocity of rain w.r.t man):

vrm = vr − vm

Shortcut: If man runs at $v_m$ and rain falls vertically at $v_r$, tan θ = vm / vr (from vertical).

Aircraft-Wind / Boat-River Drift

Actual Velocity: vactual = vengine + vwind

For Shortest Path (Zero Drift): sin θ = vw / va

5. Conceptual Insights & Rotation

Radius of Curvature (Rc)

Think of any curve as a part of a circle. The radius of that imaginary circle is given by: Rc = v⊥² / a⊥.

Example: For a projectile at max height, the velocity is purely horizontal (u cos θ) and acceleration is purely vertical (g), so Rc = (u² cos²θ) / g.

Circular Motion Accelerations

Type of Acceleration Formula Primary Function
Centripetal (ac) v² / r ONLY changes direction (acts perpendicular to velocity).
Tangential (at) dv / dt ONLY changes speed (acts parallel or anti-parallel to velocity).
Net Acceleration √(ac² + at²) anet = ac + at

Angular Kinematics (Fixed Axis)

Analogous to 1D linear motion, for constant angular acceleration (α):

ω = ω0 + αt
θ = ω0t + ½αt²
ω² = ω0² + 2αθ
Where ω is angular velocity and θ is angular displacement.

6. Common Traps & Mistakes

⚠️

Tower Projectile Trap

If you are throwing a ball from a height h, you CANNOT use the standard formula T = 2u sin θ / g. That formula strictly assumes y-displacement is zero.

Fix: You MUST use the 1D displacement equation directly: −h = (u sin θ)t − ½gt².

⚠️

Dot Product vs Cross Product Zeroes

  • If A · B = 0 ⟹ Vectors are Perpendicular (θ = 90°).
  • If A × B = 0 ⟹ Vectors are Parallel/Anti-parallel (θ = 0° or 180°).
⚠️

Confusing "Constant Velocity" in UCM

In Uniform Circular Motion (UCM), speed is constant, but velocity is constantly changing because the direction changes continuously. Hence, UCM is ALWAYS an accelerated motion.

⚠️

Adding Vectors as Scalars

3 N + 4 N is ONLY 7 N if they are perfectly parallel. It can be 1 N (anti-parallel) or 5 N (perpendicular). Always use the vector addition formula!

7. Example Applications

Extracting data from Trajectory Equation

Given: y = √3x − 5x² (in SI units).

Insight: Compare this with the trick form: y = ax − bx² ⟹ Range R = a/b.

  • Range R = √3 / 5 meters.
  • Also, tan θ = a = √3 ⟹ Angle of projection θ = 60°.

Finding a Vector Perpendicular to Two Vectors

Problem: Find a unit vector perpendicular to both A and B.

Insight: The Cross Product strictly yields a perpendicular vector. The required unit vector is n̂ = ± (A × B) / |A × B|.

8. IAT Exam Focus Points

Quick Recap / Short Notes

The IAT loves questions involving vector analysis and tricky coordinate frames. Master inclined planes and relative velocity to save crucial time.

Time of Orthogonality (Frequent IAT Model)

You are given two velocity/position vectors as functions of time, e.g., v1 = (2t)î + 4ĵ and v2 = 3î − (t)ĵ.

Question: "At what time are they perpendicular?"

Strategy: Set their dot product to zero: v1 · v2 = 0 and solve for t.

Projectile on an Inclined Plane

Tilt your axes! Make the incline the x-axis and the line perpendicular to it the y-axis. Gravity g resolves into g sin β (down the incline) and g cos β (perpendicular to the incline).

Shortcut: Time of flight on an incline (angle β) thrown at angle α relative to the incline:
T = 2u sin α / (g cos β)

Minimum Distance of Approach

When two particles are moving in 2D and you need to find the closest they get to each other:

  • Freeze one particle (make it the origin).
  • Find the relative velocity vector of the other particle.
  • The shortest distance is simply the perpendicular dropped from the origin to the relative velocity line.
