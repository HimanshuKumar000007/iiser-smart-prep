Wave Optics

Physics Unit 10
25 min read
IAT Advanced
Hot Topic

1. Core Concept & Wavefront Theory

Light propagates as wavefronts—surfaces over which the phase of the vibration is constant. The shape of a wavefront depends on the source and the distance from it.

Spherical

Produced by a Point Source. Radius increases with time.

Cylindrical

Produced by a Linear Source (like a slit).

Plane

Produced by sources at Infinity. Wavefronts are parallel planes.

Huygens Principle

1. Every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary wavelets.
2. The speed of these wavelets equals the speed of light in that medium.
3. The new wavefront at a later time is the envelope (tangential surface) of these secondary wavelets in the forward direction.

Huygens Proofs (IAT Essentials)

Reflection Proof

Using the geometric construction of wavefronts hitting a surface:

  • Time taken for wavelet from A to reach A' is same as B to B'.
  • Triangles are congruent, leading to ∠i = ∠r.

Refraction Proof

Explains why light bends when changing medium speeds:

  • v1 / sin i = v2 / sin r
  • This derives Snell's Law: n1 sin i = n2 sin r.
  • Crucial Result: λmedium = λvac / n.

2. Key Formulas

Phase & Path Difference:

Δφ = (2π / λ) Δx
Relation between Phase Difference (Δφ) and Path Difference (Δx).

Resultant Intensity:

I = I1 + I2 + 2√(I1I2) cos(Δφ)
General formula. If I1 = I2 = I0, then I = 4I0 cos2(Δφ/2).

Young’s Double Slit Experiment (YDSE):

Δx ≈ d sinθ ≈ y(d/D)
Path difference for small angles θ.
yn = nλD / d
Position of the nth Maxima (Bright) where n = 0, 1, 2...
yn = (2n - 1)λD / 2d
Position of the nth Minima (Dark) where n = 1, 2, 3...
β = λD / d
Fringe Width.

Optical Path & Glass Slab Shift:

Δx = (μ - 1)t  &implies;  Δy = (D/d)(μ - 1)t
Path difference added by a slab of thickness t, and the resulting shift of central maxima (Δy).

Single Slit Diffraction (Slit width a):

a sinθ = nλ
Condition for Minima where n = 1, 2, 3...
W = 2λD / a
Linear width of the Central Maximum.

Polarisation:

I = I0 cos2θ
Malus’s Law: Intensity transmitted by an analyzer.
tan(ip) = μ
Brewster’s Law: At the polarizing angle ip, reflected and refracted rays are exactly 90° apart.

3. Important Results

Production of Coherent Sources

Two sources are coherent if they have a constant phase difference. Since two independent sources can never be perfectly coherent, we use these methods:

  • Division of Wavefront: Passing a single wavefront through two slits (e.g., YDSE).
  • Division of Amplitude: Reflecting or refracting parts of a single beam (e.g., Newton's Rings).

Interference vs Diffraction Comparison

Feature Interference (YDSE) Diffraction (Single Slit)
Origin Superposition of two separate wavefronts. Superposition of wavelets from the same wavefront.
Fringe Intensity All bright fringes have equal intensity. Intensity decreases rapidly for higher orders.
Fringe Width All fringes have equal width (β). Central max is twice as wide as others.
Dark Fringes Perfectly dark (zero intensity). Not perfectly dark (some residual light).

4. Conceptual Insights

Energy Conservation

Interference does not destroy energy. The energy "missing" at the dark fringes is merely relocated to the bright fringes. The average intensity across the screen remains perfectly conserved at I1 + I2.

The Slab Shift

If a glass slab is placed in front of the upper slit in a YDSE setup, the wave slows down in the glass. It takes longer to reach the screen, so the central maximum (the zero time-delay point) must shift upwards (towards the slit with the slab) to compensate for the delay.

Polarisation Proof

Only transverse waves can be polarized. Since light can be polarized, it definitively proves that light is a transverse wave (longitudinal waves like sound cannot be polarized).

5. Common Mistakes

  • The "Condition Swap" Trap:
    In YDSE, d sinθ = nλ gives MAXIMA.
    In Diffraction, a sinθ = nλ gives MINIMA. Do not mix these up!
  • First Polarizer: When unpolarized light of intensity I0 passes through the very first polarizer, its intensity automatically halves to I0/2, regardless of the polarizer's angle. Malus's Law applies only when already-polarized light hits a second polarizer (analyzer).
  • Missing the Central Max in Diffraction: The first minimum occurs at sinθ = λ/a. The central maximum stretches from -λ/a to +λ/a, which is why its total angular width is 2λ/a.

6. Example Applications

Immersing YDSE setup in Water

The wavelength of light in water decreases to λ' = λ/μ. Since the fringe width is β = λD/d, the fringe width also precisely decreases by a factor of μ. The entire interference pattern shrinks tightly together.

Intensity Ratio Problems

You are given the ratio of slit widths (which equals the ratio of intensities I1/I2). You need to find Imax / Imin.

Shortcut:
Imax / Imin =[(√I1 + √I2) / (√I1 - √I2)]2

7. IAT Exam Focus Points

Fringe Shift Calculation (Very High Yield):

You will be asked how many fringes shifted when a slab of thickness t is introduced. Formula:
Number of fringes shifted (n) = (μ - 1)t / λ.

Brewster's Angle vs Critical Angle:

Do not confuse them! Brewster's Angle is for maximum polarization: tan(ip) = μ. The Critical angle is for Total Internal Reflection: sin(ic) = 1/μ.

Missing Wavelengths:

A point P is directly in front of one of the slits (y = d/2). To find the missing wavelength at P, calculate the path difference Δx = yd/D = d2/2D and equate it to the condition for minima: (n - ½)λ.

Three Polarizers:

A classic IAT question places a third polarizer at 45° between two crossed polarizers (which initially block all light). The insertion of the middle polarizer actually allows light to pass through! Calculate step-by-step using Malus's Law (I0 → I0/2 → (I0/2)cos2(45°) → ...).

8. Practice & Assessment

Quick Check

Q1. If the YDSE setup is immersed in a liquid of refractive index μ, the fringe width β becomes:

Correct! Since λ decreases to λ/μ, the fringe width β = λD/d also decreases by factor μ.

Q2. In single slit diffraction, the first minimum occurs at an angle θ such that:

Correct! The general condition for minima is a sinθ = nλ. For the first minimum, n = 1.
